# Behold: Camelot — roster and terminology

Card groups collect related elements; development does not mean that one character literally becomes another. Numbered orientations and prerequisites are game abstractions. No sequence below claims a historical chronology.

## Realms and rivals

The four realms are The Crown, The Fellowship, Arthur's Empire and The Grail Quest. The first two share Crown-era banners; the last two share Quest-era banners. Galahad must be active to enter The Grail Quest.

Opponents are Mordred, Morgan le Fay, King Lot and Lucius. Chronicle of the Realm has no rival target. The numerical formulas represent game challenges, not attributes assigned to these people in the source.

Card types are Action, Court, Holding, Ally and Provision. Scores are Renown. Materials, population, wealth, unrest and prosperity retain their functional meanings. Printed icons count wherever the active card lies unless a rule specifically limits the count to controlled Holdings.

## Source versus adaptation

The literary basis is Thomas Malory's *Le Morte d'Arthur*: [Volume I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1251/1251-h/1251-h.htm) and [Volume II](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1252/1252-h/1252-h.htm), with some spellings modernized.

Named people and places are drawn from that tradition. King Bors and Sir Bors are deliberately distinguished. The Last Battle, the Dolorous Stroke, the Grail and the Round Table refer to narrative elements.

Economic labels, the four Camelot stages, the four realm labels as a progression, Guarding the Realm, The Wounded Lands, Balin's Fatal Quest and Merlin's Counsel are our descriptive labels. They are not presented as quotations or canonical organizations. The grouping of places does not imply that they are nested territories or stages of one settlement.

All assigned abilities, costs, icons, restrictions and rival formulas are mechanical abstractions. For example, requiring King Ban before King Bors is a game prerequisite, not a claim that the legend states that requirement.
